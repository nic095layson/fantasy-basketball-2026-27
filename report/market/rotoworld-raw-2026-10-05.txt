2025-26: On the heels of a season in which he won his first Most Valu-
able Player award and helped lead Oklahoma City to its first NBA cham-
pionship, Gilgeous-Alexander had another outstanding campaign. Shoot-
ing a career-high 55.3 percent from the field, the Thunder star wasn’t far
off from his first 50/40/90 season and helped lead Oklahoma City to the
league’s best record. One of the most productive players in fantasy bas-
ketball regardless of format, Gilgeous-Alexander won NBA MVP honors for
a second consecutive season.
What’s Changed: For SGA specifically, nothing has changed, as he
remains the cornerstone in Oklahoma City. However, some members of
the supporting cast have changed, with Luguentz Dort (Atlanta), Isaiah
Joe (Detroit) and Aaron Wiggins (Atlanta) all being traded this offseason.
Outlook: While most of the conversation regarding Pick 1.1 in fantasy
drafts has focused on San Antonio’s Victor Wembanyama and Denver’s Ni-
kola Jokić, Gilgeous-Alexander is far from a “consolation prize.” He should
remain a high draft pick, slotting in right after Wemby and “The Joker.” It
would be surprising if Gilgeous-Alexander does not produce another elite
fantasy season while seeking to become the first player to win three con-
secutive MVP awards since Larry Bird (1984-86).

2025-26: Dončić’s first full season with the Lakers was one in which he
was able to truly cement his status as the franchise’s cornerstone. While
he did miss the final five games of the regular season and the entirety of
the Lakers’ postseason run with a Grade 2 left hamstring strain, the point
guard received a waiver that made him eligible for end-of-season honors.
A first-team All-NBA selection, Dončić led the league in scoring, field goals
and three-point field goals per game.
What’s Changed: The Lakers’ “big three” is no more, as LeBron

note was the team’s acquisition of Walker Kessler, who should be a better
fit alongside Dončić than Deandre Ayton was.
Outlook: No LeBron in the picture and the addition of Kessler, a legiti-
mate lob threat, may give Dončić’s assist production a boost after he aver-
aged 8.3 helpers per game in 2025-26. While some managers in nine-cat
leagues may have an issue with the turnover tally, Luka does enough in
other areas to compensate for that issue. He’ll be a top five pick in fantasy
drafts, and provided he remains relatively healthy, Dončić should not have
much trouble meeting those expectations.

2025-26: While his scoring output decreased in comparison to 2024-
25, Cunningham’s assists and steals increased last season. He earned a
second consecutive All-Star Game appearance and led the Pistons to the
best record in the East, earning first-team All-NBA honors and finishing
fifth in MVP voting. A top 20 player in nine-cat formats, Cunningham of-
fered top 10 value to managers in eight-cat formats and those willing to
punt turnovers. The end to his season may have been a disappointment,
with the Pistons being eliminated in the second round of the playoffs, but
Cunningham is becoming the player Detroit hoped he would be when they
selected him first overall in the 2021 draft.
What’s Changed: Cunningham’s status as Detroit’s cornerstone is
unchanged, but some of the options around him have. Tobias Harris moved
to San Antonio in free agency, with the Pistons signing John Collins to fill
that void. Also, Isaiah Joe was added via trade in hopes of surrounding the
star point guard with more shooting.
Outlook: Even with the turnover concerns, Cunningham should not fall
out of the first ten picks in fantasy drafts, regardless of league format.
With Collins and Joe in the fold, the hope is that he can increase the as-
sists even more after averaging a career-high 9.9 per game last season.
As long as he can remain reasonably healthy, having appeared in at least
62 games each of the last three seasons, Cunningham will be immensely
valuable to fantasy managers.

2025-26: An All-Star each of the last four seasons, Edwards missed out
on All-NBA honors because he did not hit the 65-game minimum. Howev-
er, he was highly productive when available, averaging a career-high 28.8
points per game. His rebounds and assists fell compared to the 2024-25
averages, but Edwards was more efficient as a scorer and playmaker. De-
spite a lingering knee injury, Ant-Man helped lead the Timberwolves to the
second round of the playoffs, where they were eliminated by the eventual
Western Conference champion Spurs.
What’s Changed: While Edwards’ role remains the same, some of
the options around him have changed. Minnesota acquired LaMelo Ball
from the Hornets in a multi-team deal that sent Julius Randle to Brooklyn
and Naz Reid to Charlotte. Jonathan Kuminga was signed as a free agent,
and Donte DiVincenzo will miss a significant portion of next season (if not
all of it) as he recovers from a ruptured Achilles tendon.
Outlook: Ball’s addition should make the Timberwolves more explo-
sive offensively, but it may also force the team to ask more of Edwards
defensively. If that leads to increased “stocks” production from Ant-Man,
fantasy managers won’t complain, especially if his efficiency continues
to improve. After providing second-round value in fantasy basketball last
season, Edwards is more than capable of jumping into the first round,
which is when he should be selected in most drafts.

the lingering concerns regarding Joel Embiid’s availability.

fense as often as he has been in the past. While the assists may take a hit,
there will also be more opportunities for him to attack closeouts. Maxey’s
fantasy value may take a slight hit, but he remains worthy of first-round
consideration, regardless of league format. Because even with James and
Brown in the fold, fantasy managers also have to take Embiid’s availability
issues into consideration when evaluating Maxey’s role.
